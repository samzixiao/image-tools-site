Privacy and sharing workflow

How to Cover Private Information in an Image

Screenshots and product drafts often contain more information than the audience needs. A good redaction makes the private detail unreadable in the exported image while keeping the rest of the context useful.

Identify what needs covering

Look beyond the obvious address or phone number. Order IDs, tracking codes, email addresses, QR codes, signatures, faces, license plates, barcodes, account balances, and document fragments can all reveal information that was not intended for the audience. A product draft may also show a supplier label or a shipping sticker in the background.

Decide who will see the image and what they actually need to know. A customer may need to see the product shape but not the order reference. A support report may need the error message but not a name or account number. Removing unnecessary details before sharing is safer than trying to explain them away later.

Choose the right cover style

A solid cover is the clearest choice when the information must be completely unreadable. A large opaque sticker or block can be appropriate for a public screenshot. Mosaic or blur can preserve a sense of the underlying area, but it may be unsafe if the original characters remain partly recognizable or if the covered region is small.

Decorative privacy stickers are useful for casual photos, faces, or personal details when the sticker fully covers the target. Choose a shape large enough to cover the entire region, including a small margin. Do not rely on a cute icon alone if the edges of a name, number, or face remain visible.

Why a tiny cover is not enough

Images are often viewed at different scales. A cover that seems large in the editor may become small in a compressed preview, or a viewer may zoom in. Text with high contrast can remain readable through a weak blur. The safest test is to export the image, reopen it, and zoom around the covered area. If any character, facial feature, or code can still be inferred, enlarge the cover or use a solid one.

Place the cover on its own layer when possible. This lets you move or delete it without damaging the base image and makes it easier to create a clean version for a different audience. For a permanent public export, verify the downloaded file rather than assuming the editor's selection state was included.

SizeCraft workflow

  1. Load the image locally.
    Use a copy when the original must remain untouched.
  2. Choose a cover tool.
    Select a privacy sticker, mosaic, blur, or solid cover according to the sensitivity of the information.
  3. Place it over the whole detail.
    Add a small safety margin and use the preview to position it precisely.
  4. Resize without exposing an edge.
    Keep the cover large enough for the final viewing size and check adjacent characters.
  5. Export a sharing copy.
    Keep the private original separate and reopen the export before sending it.

Multiple private details

When an image has several private areas, add separate covers rather than one giant block that hides useful context. Each cover can be moved and resized independently. Work from the top of the image downward and review the entire frame again at the end, because one hidden detail can be easy to miss when you focus on the first one.

For a collage, treat every tile as a separate risk area. A label hidden in one tile may be exposed after another tile is moved. Select the relevant tile, add the cover, then check the complete collage. The same applies to layered images: verify which layer is above the cover and whether an overlay has moved after the redaction was placed.

Do not confuse redaction with security

A redacted image protects what is visible in that rendered export. It does not make the original file safe to share or erase information from a private document. Keep originals in a controlled location, avoid uploading sensitive files to tools you do not trust, and do not post a screenshot of the unredacted source while asking for help.

If a destination requires a specific privacy or data-handling process, follow that process as well. An image editor is one step in a careful workflow, not a guarantee that every copy, cache, or message has been removed.

Redaction checklist

  • Identify names, numbers, faces, codes, and background details.
  • Choose a cover that makes the information unreadable.
  • Use a margin around the private region.
  • Check every tile and layer in a collage.
  • Export, reopen, and zoom into the final file.
  • Keep the original private and share only the redacted copy.
