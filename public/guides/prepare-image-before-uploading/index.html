Final image checklist

How to Prepare an Image Before Uploading It

A few minutes of checking before upload can prevent broken thumbnails, clipped products, unreadable text, and privacy mistakes. Use this checklist after editing and before the file leaves your computer.

Confirm the destination

Start by writing down where the image will go. A social post, video thumbnail, marketplace listing, website card, email, and print file may each need a different canvas or format. Platform requirements can change, and different categories may have additional rules, so check the current destination instructions before relying on a remembered size.

Use a SizeCraft preset as a practical starting point, then verify against the destination. The preset helps you compose; it does not guarantee that a third-party service will accept the result. If you are making several variants, name each export with its destination so a square social image does not get uploaded into a wide thumbnail slot by mistake.

Check dimensions and ratio

Verify the actual pixel width and height of the exported file. Do not assume that the preview frame or filename is enough. A file called “square” may still have a slightly different dimension if it came from another application. The ratio matters as much as the number: stretching a portrait into a landscape makes people and products look unnatural.

For a digital file, pixels describe the raster canvas. For print, you may also need physical dimensions, resolution, color profile, and bleed. If the image includes a dimension marker, make sure the number describes the real product or measurement and not merely the pixel length of a line.

Inspect the crop and subject

Look at the full image and then the small image. Faces should not be clipped at an awkward point, product edges should not disappear, and text should not sit so close to a boundary that a destination crop could remove it. A little breathing room is usually safer than a frame that touches every edge.

Check unusual details such as transparent packaging, thin cables, hair, hands, and reflective surfaces. These are common places for an automatic background treatment or crop to look artificial. If the result feels wrong, return to the source and make a deliberate adjustment rather than hiding the problem under an overlay.

Choose format and compression

JPEG is often practical for photographs, PNG is useful for transparency and crisp graphic edges, and WebP can be efficient for web workflows that support it. Choose based on the destination and the content. A format that is excellent for a transparent logo may be unnecessarily large for a photograph, while a heavily compressed JPEG can damage small text.

Open the export after saving. Look for blocky edges, color banding, a transparent background that became white, or a file that is unexpectedly large. If the file is too heavy, lower quality gradually and compare at the final viewing size. Do not judge only by zooming to an enormous scale that no viewer will use.

Remove private or unnecessary details

Before upload, inspect the background for addresses, order numbers, shipping labels, faces, screens, or documents. Cover or remove anything the audience does not need. If you used a privacy sticker or mosaic, zoom into the exported file and confirm that no characters or facial details remain visible around the edge.

Keep the source and the public export separate. A clean original is useful for future edits, but it should not be attached to a public support ticket or shared accidentally. Use a redacted copy when asking for feedback on a workflow.

SizeCraft final workflow

  1. Choose the destination preset.
    Start with a known canvas or enter custom dimensions.
  2. Crop and position the source.
    Keep the subject natural and leave a safe margin.
  3. Add only useful layers.
    Use text, shapes, dimensions, collage tiles, and privacy covers when they improve the result.
  4. Export the intended format.
    Use a clear destination-based filename.
  5. Reopen and inspect.
    Check actual dimensions, color, text, edges, and private areas outside the editor.

Use a repeatable filename system

A predictable filename is a small but useful quality control. Include the subject, destination, dimensions, and version when needed. For example, blue-mug-amazon-2000x2000-v2.jpg tells you more than final-final-new.jpg. If a customer or teammate needs a correction, a clear version number makes it easier to identify which file changed.

Do not let a filename replace the actual check. A correct name can still point to a wrong export. Treat it as a way to organize files after the visual and technical checks have passed.

Pre-upload checklist

  • The destination and current requirements are known.
  • The exported pixel dimensions and ratio are correct.
  • The crop does not cut off the subject or important labels.
  • The format and file weight fit the destination.
  • Text and dimension labels remain readable.
  • Private information is covered in the actual export.
  • The download opens normally and has a clear filename.
