Shape crop workflow

How to Use Shape Crop for Profile Images and Highlights

Shape crops are useful when a normal rectangle does not communicate the intended design. The important part is keeping the source image, crop boundary, and shape scale aligned so a circle stays round and the subject remains visible.

Choose a shape for the job

A circle is a dependable choice for avatars, headshots, and simple highlight graphics. An ellipse can work when the design has a deliberate horizontal or vertical emphasis, but it should not be used to disguise a stretched photo. A heart or star can add a playful tone to a personal project, a birthday card, or a campaign graphic. Hexagons, badges, and sticker-like frames are useful when the shape itself is part of the visual language.

Start with the simplest shape that supports the content. A decorative boundary should not make a product, face, or logo harder to recognize. If the image needs to look neutral across several destinations, keep a clean rectangular export as well as the shaped version.

Why shape crops become distorted

Many shape problems come from treating a rectangular tile as if it were a square. If the same width and height values are stretched independently to fill a wide or tall slot, a circle becomes an ellipse and a star may look flattened. Another problem occurs when the shape is clipped by the preview boundary because the scale is larger than the available frame.

Use proportional scaling for the shape itself. The photo can be zoomed or moved inside the frame, while the shape boundary keeps its own ratio. Check the result at the edges of the preview and after export. A correct preview is helpful, but the saved file is the final authority.

Prepare the image for a shape

Before selecting a shape, decide what part of the image needs emphasis. For a face, keep the eyes and expression away from the edge and leave room around hair or hats. For a product, find the smallest square region that still explains the product. For a logo, make sure the mark has enough margin to avoid clipping fine strokes.

A shape crop is not the same as shrinking the whole image. Move the source inside the frame until the desired region is visible. If the source is too small, do not enlarge it until important details become soft. Choose a cleaner source or accept a simpler crop instead.

SizeCraft step-by-step workflow

  1. Load the source image.
    Keep the original file available so you can make another crop later.
  2. Choose the output canvas.
    A square canvas is a useful default for circles, stars, badges, and profile graphics.
  3. Select one shape.
    Click the shape tool once to activate it and again to cancel it when the clean rectangle is better.
  4. Position the photo.
    Drag the image inside the frame and use zoom to place the subject without stretching.
  5. Adjust shape scale.
    Scale the boundary independently where the design needs more or less margin.
  6. Export and inspect.
    Check the corners, transparent or background areas, and subject proportions in the download.

Making a circle, ellipse, and badge look correct

For a circle, use equal width and height and keep the center of the subject near the center of the shape. If the circle is placed inside a rectangular collage slot, leave the rectangular slot as the outer boundary and use an internal square shape region. For an ellipse, choose the direction intentionally and make sure the image itself remains natural even if the boundary is oval.

A badge often has points or curved shoulders that need extra space near the outer boundary. Avoid pushing the shape so close to the tile edge that the points disappear. If a shape looks incomplete, reduce the shape scale or give the collage slot more breathing room before changing the source crop.

Shapes with collage and layers

When using a collage, each tile is its own small composition. Upload the image to the intended tile, then select that tile before choosing a shape. The tile's photo position and its shape scale serve different purposes: moving the photo chooses what part is shown; scaling the shape changes the boundary. Keep those controls separate so a small product photo does not accidentally become smaller just because the mask changed.

For layered images, decide which object is in front before you export. A shape crop can sit above a background image, while text or a dimension marker can sit above both. Select the layer you want to adjust, make one change, and click outside it to review the clean composition.

Shape crop checklist

  • Circle and other proportional shapes keep their intended ratio.
  • The source photo is moved inside the shape instead of stretched.
  • The subject has a safe margin around important edges.
  • The shape is fully visible inside its canvas or collage tile.
  • The saved export matches the preview and opens normally.
